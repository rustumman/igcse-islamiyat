The Qur’an and the Sunnah

2.3 The Qur’an in Legal Thinking · why the Qur’an leaves practical gaps for the Sunnah to fill

The previous lesson's prediction question asked where the missing detail of how to pray comes from. The Qur’an states its own relationship to the Prophet's example directly:

“Whatever the Messenger has given you, take it; and whatever he has forbidden you, refrain from.” Qur’an 59:7

The gap between principle and practice is not an oversight — it is deliberate design. A book meant to be recited and revered as unchanging divine speech is not the ideal format for procedural detail that a living, breathing teacher can demonstrate, correct, and adapt in real time. The Prophet's own life — witnessed, memorised and passed on by thousands of companions — became the working manual for applying the Qur’an's principles, which is exactly why the Hadith sciences you may study elsewhere in this course (isnad, matn, the classification into Sahih, Hasan and Da’if) matter so much: a legal system built partly on Hadith is only as reliable as its verification of which hadiths are authentic.
