Preservation of the Qur’an

2.1 The Revelation of the Qur’an · how the text was safeguarded during the Prophet's own lifetime

Even though receiving revelation could be physically overwhelming, the text itself was carefully safeguarded from the very start — well before it was ever gathered into a single book. This lesson is the bridge into Unit 2.2, which covers what happened after the Prophet's death.

1

Written down by literate companions

The Prophet appointed scribes of revelation, who recorded each verse as it came — on whatever material was available: pieces of leather, flat stones, the shoulder-bones of camels, and palm-leaf stalks.

2

Recorded in the order it arrived, not the order it was later arranged

Because revelation came down in portions responding to specific situations across 23 years, verses were not written — or later arranged in the mus-haf — strictly in the order they were revealed.

3

Checked annually with Jibreel

Every Ramadan the Prophet recited the whole of the Qur’an revealed so far to Jibreel, who reviewed it with him — a check on accuracy that happened twice in the final Ramadan of his life.

4

Not yet a single book

What did not exist yet was one bound volume gathering every verse together in a fixed order — that step only came after the Prophet's death, once revelation itself had ended (covered in full in Unit 2.2).

□ Common mix-up to avoid

Don't confuse the Prophet's yearly review of the Qur’an with Jibreel during his own lifetime with the later event where companions compared their written copies against each other. That second event — triggered by Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman's warning about regional recitation disputes — belongs to ’Uthman's standardisation in Unit 2.2, roughly two decades after the Prophet's death, not to the preservation process described here.
