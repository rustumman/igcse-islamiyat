First Revelation

2.1 The Revelation of the Qur’an · Ramadan 610 CE · Cave of Hira, Makkah

For twenty-three years, a message arrived to one man in fragments — sometimes five verses, sometimes a single phrase. Understanding how that process began, on one specific night in a cave outside Makkah, is worth as many marks as understanding what was revealed.

□ Predict before you read

Every scripture Muslims believe came before the Qur’an — the Tawrah given to Musa, the Injeel given to ‘Isa — is described as having been handed down essentially complete. The Qur’an alone arrived in pieces, over more than two decades, often verse by verse in response to a specific event. Before reading on: write down two reasons you think that difference might exist. You'll meet the examiner's version of your answer in the next lesson — compare notes.

What exactly is ‘Wahy’?

Muslims call the process by which God communicated the Qur’an to the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) al-wahy — inspiration, or divine communication. It was not a new phenomenon invented for him: the Qur’an states that God has always spoken to His chosen messengers this way, naming Nuh, Ibrahim, Isma’il, Ishaq, Ya’qub, ’Isa, Ayyub and others as recipients of wahy before him (Qur’an 4:163–165), and describing the general principle directly:

“It is not for any human being that Allah should speak to him except by revelation, or from behind a veil, or that He sends a messenger to reveal, by His permission, what He wills.” Qur’an 42:51

That single verse is effectively the syllabus for this section: it names the three channels through which revelation reached a prophet, and Muhammad (pbuh) is the only messenger in Islamic belief to have experienced all three. So central was this connection that when he died in 11 AH, his household's freed servant Umm Ayman was found weeping. Asked whether she wept because the Prophet had died and moved to something better, she replied that she knew that — she wept because revelation from the sky had now stopped. Abu Bakr and ’Umar wept with her.

The night in the cave

By the age of forty, Muhammad (pbuh) had developed a habit of withdrawing for days at a time to a small cave on Jabal al-Nur, a mountain just outside Makkah, to reflect in solitude. It was there, in Ramadan of the year that would come to be counted as 610 CE, that Jibreel appeared to him for the first time and issued a single command: اقرأ — “Iqra”, “Read.”

Ramadan, 610 CE

Jibreel commands “Iqra” — three times

The Prophet, who could not read, replied each time, “I am not one who reads.” Each time Jibreel embraced him tightly until he reached the limit of his endurance, then released him and repeated the command.

Same night

The first five verses are given

Jibreel then recited what became the opening of Surah al-’Alaq (96:1–5): “Read in the name of your Lord who created — created man from a clinging substance. Read, and your Lord is the Most Generous — who taught by the pen — taught man that which he knew not.”

Same night

He returns home trembling

He hurried to his wife Khadijah and said, “Cover me! Cover me!” — convinced something terrible had happened to him.

Same night

Khadijah's reassurance

She answered him without hesitation, before either of them understood what had occurred.

Days later

Waraqah ibn Nawfal confirms it

Khadijah's elderly cousin, a Christian familiar with the earlier scriptures, identifies the visitor and predicts what is coming.

□ In her own words — Khadijah bint Khuwaylid

“Never! By Allah, Allah will never disgrace you. You keep good relations with your kith and kin, help the poor and the destitute, serve your guests generously, and assist those afflicted by calamity.”

She then took him to her cousin, Waraqah ibn Nawfal, an elderly man who had studied the Christian scriptures. On hearing the account, Waraqah said:

“This is the Namus [the same one who keeps secrets, i.e. Jibreel] that Allah sent down to Musa. I wish I were young, and could live up to the time when your people will turn you out.” The Prophet asked, “Will they turn me out?” Waraqah replied, “Yes — never has a man brought something similar to what you have brought without being treated with hostility. If I live to see that day, I will support you strongly.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 3, narrated by ’A’ishah

□ Elaborate — explain it in your own words

Three different people responded to the same event in three different ways: the Prophet with fear, Khadijah with immediate practical faith, Waraqah with scriptural recognition and an accurate prediction of persecution to come. Without looking back at the text, explain out loud (or on paper) why each of their three reactions matters as evidence for Muslims that the revelation was genuine. This kind of reasoning is exactly what an AO2 “(b) part” question is testing.

The pause in revelation, and the second wahy

After that first night in the cave, revelation stopped. Scholars call this gap al-fatra — reports differ on exactly how long it lasted — and the Prophet (pbuh) was left anxious and longing for it to return.

“While I was walking, I heard a voice from the sky. I looked up, and there was the same angel who had come to me in Hira, sitting on a throne between the sky and the earth. I was terrified of him and went back home…” Prophet Muhammad (pbuh), narrated in Sahih al-Bukhari

This time Jibreel appeared in his true, enormous angelic form, filling the horizon wherever the Prophet turned his gaze, and identified himself directly: “O Muhammad, you are the Messenger of Allah, and I am Jibreel.” The fear passed, the fatra ended, and shortly afterwards Surah al-Muddaththir (74:1–5) was revealed — “O you who is covered! Arise and warn…” — formally launching his public mission as a warner.

PAST PAPER · 0493/11 · PAPER 1, Q2

(a) Describe the Prophet's (pbuh) experience of receiving the first revelation, including the roles of Jibreel, Khadijah and Waraqah ibn Nawfal. 10 marks

A close relative of this question has appeared in May/June 2022, May/June 2023 and Nov 2025. It wants the narrative of the very first revelation, not a general classification of revelation-types — naming Jibreel, Khadijah or Waraqah, or saying “the first revelation,” is the signal to switch to this answer.

AO1 · model answer points

  1. Context: at forty years old, the Prophet regularly withdrew to the Cave of Hira on Jabal al-Nur to reflect in solitude.
  2. Jibreel appeared and commanded “Iqra” (“Read”) three times, embracing him tightly each time the Prophet said he could not read.
  3. Jibreel then recited the first five verses of Surah al-’Alaq (96:1–5).
  4. The Prophet returned home trembling, saying “Cover me! Cover me!”, convinced something terrible had happened to him.
  5. Khadijah reassured him immediately and without hesitation, citing his honesty, kindness and generosity as reasons Allah would not disgrace him.
  6. She took him to her cousin Waraqah ibn Nawfal, who identified the visitor as the Namus — the same angel who came to Musa — and predicted the Prophet would face hostility from his own people.

Part (b) · AO2 — 4 marks, two recurring angles

Angle 1 (J23 s11): “Why is it important the revelation came to a prophet who could not read or write?” An unlettered man producing language this eloquent and structured is presented by Muslims as evidence the words could not be his own composition — strengthening the claim that the Qur’an is divine speech, not human authorship.

Angle 2 (N25 w11): “Why is it significant that God sent the first revelation through an angel?” Qur’an 42:51 states no human speaks with God except by revelation, from behind a veil, or through a sent messenger — using an angel let the Prophet receive the message directly and repeatedly, in the same form earlier prophets such as Musa had received it, linking his prophethood to theirs.

Examiner pattern: candidates who prepared only the “different ways revelation came” answer (see the next lesson) often reuse it here and lose marks for not telling the actual story. Prepare both answers separately, and check which one the question wording is actually asking for before writing.
