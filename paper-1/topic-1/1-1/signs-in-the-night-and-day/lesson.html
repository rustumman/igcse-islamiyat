Signs in the Night, the Day, and the Sky

1.1 Allah in Himself · Paper 1, Topic 1

Every sunrise and sunset is, according to the Qur’an, a daily reminder pointing straight past the sun itself toward the One who made it — and Surah Fussilat makes that point with a direct command.

Surah Fussilat, verse 37, uses the most familiar cycle in human experience — night turning to day, the sun and the moon crossing the sky — to make a single sharp theological point: these things are signs (ayat) of Allah’s power, not objects of worship in their own right.

Signs, not deities

Ibn Kathir explains that the night, the day, the sun and the moon are mentioned because “by the variations in their movements man may know the stages of night and day, of weeks, months and years” — they are useful, predictable, and awe-inspiring, which is exactly why some ancient peoples were tempted to worship them directly instead of the One who set them in motion.

The direct command: do not prostrate to the sun or moon

The verse gives an explicit instruction: do not prostrate to the sun, and do not prostrate to the moon — prostrate to Allah, who created them. Ibn Kathir stresses that because the sun and moon are themselves created and entirely under Allah’s dominion, worship must go only to their Creator: “do not associate anything in worship with Him, for your worship of Him will be of no benefit to you if you worship others alongside Him.”

Rejecting sun and moon worship specifically

This verse is a direct, named rejection of an ancient and widespread form of shirk — treating the sun and moon (or the “signs” and cycles they produce, such as astrology) as sources of power or fate in their own right, rather than as created objects entirely subject to Allah’s will.

If people refuse, Allah loses nothing

Ibn Kathir also connects the passage to the idea that those who reject this message cannot diminish Allah’s glory in any way — the angels around Him glorify Him constantly regardless of what happens on earth. The same creative power that governs day and night is, Ibn Kathir notes, also the power capable of resurrecting the dead.

“And among His signs are the night and the day, and the sun and the moon. Do not prostrate to the sun or to the moon, but prostrate to Allah who created them, if it is Him that you worship.” Qur’an 41:37

How to unpack this verse for full marks

1

Name the signs

night, day, sun, moon — as evidence of Allah’s power and precise design (used for timekeeping: days, weeks, months, years).

2

State the command directly

not to prostrate to the sun or moon, but to Allah alone who created them.

3

Name the underlying theme

Tawheed al-Uluhiyyah (monotheism in worship): created things, however impressive, are never valid objects of worship.

□ Common mix-up to avoid

Don’t confuse this verse with Surah ash-Shams (91) or general “signs in creation” verses elsewhere in the Qur’an. Fussilat 41:37 is distinctive for its explicit prohibition — “do not prostrate to the sun or moon” — which is the specific detail examiners look for, not just a vague mention of “signs in nature.”

□ Elaborate

The sun and moon are things people can actually see and rely on every day, while Allah cannot be seen. Using only the reasoning in this verse, explain to someone why something visible, reliable, and powerful (like the sun) still should not be worshipped, while Allah — unseen — should be.

PAST PAPER · 0493/11 · Paper 1, Q1

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) 4 marks · (b) 4 marks

This passage has been offered as one of the three options in: May/June 2018, May/June 2019, May/June 2021, Oct/Nov 2024, Oct/Nov 2025.

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) Briefly describe the main theme(s) in each passage. [4] (b) Briefly explain the importance of these themes in a Muslim’s life today. [4]

AO1 · part (a) model answer points

  1. The night, day, sun, and moon are named as signs (ayat) of Allah’s power and design.
  2. An explicit command not to prostrate to the sun or the moon.
  3. The command to prostrate to Allah alone, who created the sun and moon.

AO2 · part (b) — importance today

Importance today — reminds Muslims that even the most reliable, awe-inspiring parts of creation are still only creation, guarding against modern forms of the same error (e.g. superstition, astrology, nature-worship); the day/night cycle is a constant, built-in reminder to reflect on Allah rather than only the things He made.

Examiner note: Candidates sometimes write generally about “signs in nature” without ever stating the specific command in the verse (not to prostrate to sun/moon). Examiners reward candidates who quote or closely paraphrase the actual prohibition.
