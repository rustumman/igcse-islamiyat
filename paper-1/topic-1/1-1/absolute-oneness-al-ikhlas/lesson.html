Absolute Oneness — Surah al-Ikhlas

1.1 Allah in Himself · Paper 1, Topic 1

Four short verses, and the Prophet (pbuh) said reciting them is worth a third of the entire Qur’an — because they answer, once and for all, the question “what is Allah like?”

□ Predict before you read

Before reading on: if someone asked you to describe Allah in exactly four short sentences, with no room for elaboration, what four ideas would you choose to include? Compare your answer with what Surah al-Ikhlas actually says.

Surah al-Ikhlas (112) is one of the shortest surahs in the Qur’an, and one of the most important — a direct, compressed answer to a question the Prophet (pbuh) was repeatedly asked.

Passage in Arabic — for reference only, not required for the exam

قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ (١) اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ (٢) لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ (٣) وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ (٤) Qur’an 112:1–4 (Uthmani script). The exam sets this passage in both Arabic and English but assesses your answer in English — you do not need to read, write, or transliterate the Arabic to succeed in this topic. It is included here to match the authentic source text.

Below is the English translation this lesson (and the exam) works from.

Occasion of revelation

Ibn Kathir records, via Ikrimah, that this surah was revealed in response to various groups’ false claims about Allah’s nature: some Jews of Madinah claimed Ezra (’Uzayr) was Allah’s son, Christians claimed Isa (Jesus) was His son, Zoroastrians worshipped celestial bodies, and Makkan polytheists worshipped idols. People asked the Prophet to describe his Lord’s lineage and nature, and this surah was the answer.

“Say: He is Allah, Ahad”

Al-Ahad means Allah is absolutely One — singular, with no peer, no partner, no equal, and no one who assists Him. Ibn Kathir explains the term applies exclusively and completely to Allah because of His perfection in every attribute and action.

“Allah, as-Samad”

As-Samad — “the Self-Sufficient Master” — is explained, according to Ibn Abbas (as recorded by Ibn Kathir), as “the One Who all of the creation depends upon for their needs.” As-Samad also describes a Master whose control is complete, and who is perfect in sovereignty, nobility, magnificence, forbearance, knowledge, and wisdom.

“He begets not, nor is He begotten”

This directly rejects the idea of Allah having any offspring or any origin — He was not born from anything, and nothing was born from Him. This is a direct answer to the claims mentioned in the occasion of revelation.

“And there is none comparable to Him”

The final verse closes the surah by ruling out any equal, likeness, or comparison to Allah in any respect at all.

A third of the Qur’an

Ibn Kathir records the well-known hadith, found in Sahih al-Bukhari (5015, narrated by Abu Sa’id al-Khudri) and Sahih Muslim (812a, narrated by Abu Hurayrah), where the Prophet (pbuh) said this surah “is equal to one-third of the Qur’an” — because the Qur’an’s content is traditionally divided into knowledge of Allah (Tawheed), rulings/law, and stories/accounts, and Surah al-Ikhlas covers the first of these three completely and directly.

Revision-notes checklist (Main Theme + Importance)

This lesson explicitly covers every point from the standard syllabus revision-notes summary for this passage: that this is Surah al-Ikhlas; its focus on Tawheed; Allah’s uniqueness as eternal with no family; and that His creations are not comparable to Him. On importance: it reminds Muslims not to think Allah has any helper or family; it helps them worship Him as unique; it eliminates all forms and degrees of shirk; and it helps Muslims develop a strong sense of Allah’s constant presence and knowledge.

Further virtues of this surah (fada’il, from authentic hadith)

Beyond the “one-third of the Qur’an” hadith above, two further authentic virtues are reported for Surah al-Ikhlas:

  • “Allah loves you” — a man from the Ansar used to lead his companions in prayer and would always end his recitation with Surah al-Ikhlas. When his companions asked him why, he said he loved it because it describes the Most Merciful. The Prophet (pbuh) told him: “Inform him that Allah loves him.” (Sahih al-Bukhari 774, Sahih Muslim 813)
  • A house in Paradise for reciting it ten times — the Prophet (pbuh) said: “Whoever recites Qul Huwa Allahu Ahad ten times, Allah will build for him a palace in Paradise.” (Recorded in Musnad Ahmad; graded sahih by Al-Albani in Silsilat al-Ahadith as-Sahihah, 589)
“Say: He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Self-Sufficient Master. He begets not, nor is He begotten. And there is none comparable to Him.” Qur’an 112:1–4 (English translation)

□ Where this comes from

Based on Tafsir Ibn Kathir’s commentary on Surah al-Ikhlas, including Ikrimah’s report on the occasion of revelation and Ibn Abbas’s explanation of As-Samad. The “one-third of the Qur’an” hadith is recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari (5015) and Sahih Muslim (812a), both graded Sahih (authentic).

□ Elaborate

Surah al-Ikhlas answers a question about what Allah IS by mostly stating what He is NOT — not begotten, not begetting, without equal. Why might describing Allah this way (through negation) be a safer and clearer method than trying to describe Him only through positive comparisons to things we already know?

PAST PAPER · 0493/11 · Paper 1, Q1

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) 4 marks · (b) 4 marks

This passage has been offered as one of the three options in: Oct/Nov 2021, Oct/Nov 2023.

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) Briefly describe the main theme(s) in each passage. [4] (b) Briefly explain the importance of these themes in a Muslim’s life today. [4]

AO1 · part (a) model answer points

  1. Allah’s absolute oneness (Ahad) — no partner, no equal.
  2. Allah as as-Samad — the Self-Sufficient Master all creation depends on.
  3. Rejection of Allah having any offspring or origin, and that nothing is comparable to Him.

AO2 · part (b) — importance today

Importance today — this surah is the clearest possible defence against shirk in everyday belief and speech; many Muslims recite it in every prayer and are taught it is worth a third of the Qur’an, making it central to how Tawheed is taught to children; it gives Muslims a precise, memorable answer whenever asked to describe Allah, including in interfaith conversations.

Official mark scheme wording · verbatim, CIE 0493/11 MS, Oct/Nov 2021

(a) “this is the main sura revealed to describe tawhid” and “He has no partners or family, does not have anyone to share His authority.” (b) “The importance of stressing the Oneness of God is so that people do not take other people or created things as their Lord.”

Preparation note (not from an official examiner report — a study tip based on this passage’s content): Candidates often mention “Tawheed” as the theme but forget to explain both Al-Ahad and As-Samad separately — the mark scheme rewards candidates who can distinguish “no partner” (Ahad) from “self-sufficient, depended upon by all” (As-Samad) as two related but different ideas.
