Allah, the Originator of the Heavens and Earth

1.1 Allah in Himself · Paper 1, Topic 1

If Allah has no partner in creating the universe, the Qur’an asks, how could He possibly have a partner in anything else — including a son?

Surah al-An’am, verses 101–103, builds a tightly logical argument for Allah’s uniqueness, moving from His act of creation to a direct refutation of the idea that He has offspring, and closing with a statement about the limits of human perception.

Badi’ as-Samawati wal-Ard — the Originator of the heavens and earth

The passage opens by naming Allah Badi’ — the Originator. Ibn Kathir explains that Badi’ describes Allah as the One who “originated, created, invented and brought them into existence without precedence” — He did not copy or improve on any earlier model, because there was nothing before Him to copy.

“How could He have a son, when He has no consort?”

The verse then asks a rhetorical question: how could Allah have a child when He has no consort (wife/partner)? Ibn Kathir’s reasoning is direct: “the child is the offspring of two compatible spouses.” Since nothing in creation is comparable or equal to Allah, He can have no partner of any kind — and therefore no child in the way creation understands the term. This directly answers three groups mentioned elsewhere in the Qur’an: those who said Ezra (Uzayr) was Allah’s son, those who said Isa (Jesus) was Allah’s son, and the Makkan pagans who called angels “daughters of Allah.”

Creator of all things, Knower of all things

“He is the Creator of all things” — nothing exists that Allah did not bring into being, so nothing can be His equal or His partner. The passage links creation directly to knowledge: the One who made everything necessarily knows everything about it, “He is the All-Knower of everything.”

“Vision comprehends Him not, but He comprehends all vision”

Verse 103 states that human sight cannot grasp or fully perceive Allah, while Allah’s own perception (of everything, including human sight itself) is total and complete. This is a key verse Muslim scholars use to explain why Allah cannot be seen in this worldly life, while still affirming that He sees and perceives everything without limit — the relationship of perception runs entirely in His favour.

“He is the Originator of the heavens and the earth. How can He have a son when He has no consort? He created all things, and He is the All-Knower of everything... No vision can grasp Him, but He grasps all vision. He is the Most Subtle, the All-Aware.” Qur’an 6:101–103

What false beliefs this passage refutes

  • Ezra (Uzayr) as “son of Allah” (a claim attributed to some Jews of Madinah)
  • Isa (Jesus) as “son of Allah” (a Christian doctrine)
  • Angels as “daughters of Allah” (a Makkan pagan belief)

What the passage affirms instead

  • Allah alone originated all of creation, with no precedent and no model
  • Allah has no consort, so the very idea of Him having offspring is logically impossible
  • Allah’s knowledge and perception of everything is total, while human perception of Him is limited

□ Where this comes from

Based on Tafsir Ibn Kathir’s commentary on Surah al-An’am 6:101–103, which explains Badi’ as creation “without precedence,” grounds the rejection of a “son” in Allah having no consort, and reads verse 103 as establishing both the limits of human vision and the completeness of Allah’s own perception.

□ Elaborate

The passage’s argument against Allah having a son is built on ordinary human logic (a child needs two parents), not just a bare command to believe. Why might the Qur’an choose to argue this way — with reasoning a listener could follow and check for themselves — rather than simply stating “Allah has no son, believe it”? Think about who this passage was originally addressing.

PAST PAPER · 0493/11 · Paper 1, Q1

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) 4 marks · (b) 4 marks

This passage has been offered as one of the three options in: May/June 2016, May/June 2018, May/June 2022, Oct/Nov 2025.

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) Briefly describe the main theme(s) in each passage. [4] (b) Briefly explain the importance of these themes in a Muslim’s life today. [4]

AO1 · part (a) model answer points

  1. Allah as Badi’ — the Originator of the heavens and earth, with no precedent.
  2. Refutation of Allah having a son, since He has no consort/equal.
  3. Allah’s total knowledge of creation and the limits of human vision compared to His own perception.

AO2 · part (b) — importance today

Importance today — protects Muslims from beliefs that compromise Tawheed (e.g. treating any prophet or being as divine); reassures believers that Allah’s knowledge of them is total and unmatched by any human understanding of Him; grounds the Muslim rejection of the Trinity and similar doctrines in reasoned argument, not just assertion.

Examiner note: A common error is to summarise this passage only as “Allah has no son” without explaining the reasoning given (no consort) or mentioning the Originator/knowledge themes — examiners want the full logical structure, not just the conclusion.
