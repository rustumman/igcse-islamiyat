Ayat al-Kursi — The Throne Verse

1.1 Allah in Himself · Paper 1, Topic 1

One single verse of the Qur’an is called “the greatest verse in the Book of Allah” by the Prophet (pbuh) himself — and it is the single most-examined passage in this whole topic, having appeared in Paper 1 six times in the last ten years.

□ Predict before you read

Before you read on: what do you think it would mean for a being to never sleep, never tire, and know everything that has ever happened and ever will? Jot down one word you’d use to describe that kind of existence — you’ll compare it with the verse’s own vocabulary below.

Ayat al-Kursi (“the Verse of the Throne/Footstool”) is verse 255 of Surah al-Baqarah, and is widely regarded by Muslims as the single greatest verse in the Qur’an. It is a concentrated statement of Tawheed — the oneness of Allah — built entirely out of Allah’s own names and attributes, with no address to human beings at all until its final clause.

The verse opens with “Allah — there is no god but He”, the clearest possible statement that worship belongs to Allah alone. It then names Him Al-Hayy (the Ever-Living) and Al-Qayyum (the Self-Sustaining, Self-Subsisting). Ibn Kathir explains that Al-Hayy means Allah “never dies” and sustains all creation while remaining entirely self-sufficient, while Al-Qayyum means that “all creation stands in need of Allah and totally relies on Him” — the relationship runs in one direction only.

Neither slumber nor sleep seizes Him

The verse next says “neither drowsiness nor sleep overtakes Him.” Ibn Kathir explains this negates any “shortcoming, unawareness or ignorance” in Allah — a builder who fell asleep could let his building collapse; Allah’s control over the universe never lapses for even a moment.

His knowledge and His Kursi

“To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth.” Everything that exists is His possession and under His command. No one can intercede with Him except by His permission — removing any notion that lesser beings (angels, saints, ancestors) can act as independent go-betweens. Allah’s knowledge covers what is “before them and what will be after them,” and human beings grasp none of that knowledge “except for what He wills” — knowledge is a gift He chooses to give, not something creation possesses by right.

The verse then describes His Kursi as extending over the heavens and the earth. Ibn Kathir records that Ibn Abbas explained that if the seven heavens and seven earths were flattened out, they would be “as a ring in a desert, compared to the Kursi” — a deliberate image of how small all of creation is next to Allah.

No fatigue in preserving all of it

The verse closes: “And their preservation tires Him not.” Sustaining the entire universe, at every moment, causes Allah no burden at all — in total contrast to any created being, for whom guarding even one thing for a long time becomes exhausting.

“Allah! There is no god but He, the Ever-Living, the Self-Subsisting, Sustainer of all that exists. Neither slumber nor sleep overtakes Him... His Kursi extends over the heavens and the earth, and He feels no fatigue in guarding and preserving them. And He is the Most High, the Most Great.” Qur’an 2:255

Why Ayat al-Kursi is treated as exam-critical, not just a “nice verse”

1

It is the densest single-verse statement of Tawheed in the Qur’an

almost every major divine attribute tested elsewhere in Unit 1.1 (life, self-sufficiency, knowledge, power, sovereignty) also appears compressed into this one verse.

2

It carries its own authenticated virtue-hadith

which examiners expect in a strong part (b) answer, not just a description of the text.

3

It is the most frequently set passage in the last decade

(6 out of 20 sessions) — a student who has not mastered it is taking a real risk in the exam.

□ Where this comes from

This lesson follows Tafsir Ibn Kathir’s commentary on Surah al-Baqarah 2:255, which explains each name/attribute in turn and records Ibn Abbas’s report on the Kursi. The hadith on the verse’s status as “the greatest ayah” is recorded by Imam Ahmad (from Ubayy ibn Ka’b); the hadith on nightly protection from Shaytan is recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari (narrated by Abu Hurayrah).

□ Elaborate

Ayat al-Kursi never once addresses “you” (the reader) until the very last line, and even then only to describe Allah’s greatness, not to give a command. Why do you think a verse built almost entirely out of Allah’s own attributes — rather than instructions to believers — is still considered so important “in a Muslim’s life today”? Try to answer this without simply repeating “because it teaches Tawheed” — what does knowing these specific attributes (never sleeping, never fatigued, all-knowing) actually change about how a person behaves day to day?

PAST PAPER · 0493/11 · Paper 1, Q1

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) 4 marks · (b) 4 marks

This passage has been offered as one of the three options in: May/June 2016, May/June 2017, May/June 2019, Oct/Nov 2020, Oct/Nov 2023, May/June 2025.

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) Briefly describe the main theme(s) in each passage. [4] (b) Briefly explain the importance of these themes in a Muslim’s life today. [4]

AO1 · part (a) model answer points

  1. Tawheed / Allah’s absolute oneness — “there is no god but He.”
  2. Allah’s eternal life and self-sufficiency (Al-Hayy, Al-Qayyum) — He depends on nothing, everything depends on Him.
  3. Allah’s total, unceasing knowledge and power — nothing is hidden from Him and preserving all creation causes Him no fatigue.

AO2 · part (b) — importance today

Importance today — Muslims recite it after prayer and before sleep for protection; it builds constant awareness (muraqabah) that nothing is hidden from Allah, discouraging sin even in private; it reassures Muslims that Allah is never too tired or distant to answer them, so they turn to Him directly rather than to intermediaries.

Examiner note: Candidates often describe the verse’s content accurately in part (a) but repeat the same generic sentence (“Muslims should worship Allah”) for part (b) regardless of which passage was chosen. For Ayat al-Kursi specifically, tie part (b) to the verse’s own vocabulary — protection, constant awareness, direct connection to Allah — not a one-size-fits-all answer.
