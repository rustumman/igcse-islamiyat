Creator and Sustainer

1.2 Allah’s Relationship with the Created World · Surah al-Baqarah 2:21–22

Before this passage ever tells anyone what to believe, it first walks through what Allah has already done for them — food, water, shelter — and only then asks for worship in return.

Surah al-Baqarah, verses 21–22, is one of the clearest statements in the Qur’an of Tawheed al-Uluhiyyah — the oneness of Allah in worship — built entirely on the evidence of what Allah provides.

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ اعْبُدُوا رَبَّكُمُ الَّذِي خَلَقَكُمْ وَالَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ · الَّذِي جَعَلَ لَكُمُ الْأَرْضَ فِرَاشًا وَالسَّمَاءَ بِنَاءً وَأَنزَلَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً فَأَخْرَجَ بِهِ مِنَ الثَّمَرَاتِ رِزْقًا لَّكُمْ ۖ فَلَا تَجْعَلُوا لِلَّهِ أَندَادًا وَأَنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ “O mankind! Worship your Lord, who created you and those before you, so that you may attain Taqwa — who made the earth a resting place for you and the sky a canopy, and sent down rain from the sky, then brought forth thereby fruits as provision for you. So do not set up rivals to Allah while you know [the truth].” Qur’an 2:21–22 — Arabic given for reference only; the exam assesses the English translation, not Arabic recall

“O mankind, worship your Lord”

The passage opens with a direct call to all of humanity — not just believers — to worship their Lord, who created them and those before them, so that they may attain Taqwa (God-consciousness, mindfulness of Allah). Ibn Kathir explains this establishes that worship is owed to Allah specifically because He is the Creator; the command to worship follows logically from the fact of creation, not the other way around.

Earth as a resting place, sky as a canopy

Verse 22 describes Allah as the One who “made the earth a resting place for you, and the sky as a canopy,” and who sends down rain from the sky and brings forth fruits as sustenance. Ibn Kathir reads this as evidence built from ordinary, everyday experience — ground stable enough to live on, a sky that doesn’t fall, water that produces food — presented as proof of a Creator’s deliberate design and care, not accident.

What Allah has done · verse 22

  • Made the earth stable and habitable (“a resting place”)
  • Made the sky like a protective canopy
  • Sends rain and produces fruit as sustenance

What is asked in return · verses 21–22

  • Worship Allah alone, since He is the Creator
  • Aim for Taqwa (God-consciousness) through that worship
  • Do not set up rivals or partners to Allah, knowingly

The warning against rivals

The passage then commands: “So do not set up rivals to Allah while you know [the truth].” Ibn Kathir includes a hadith in which the Prophet (pbuh) was asked which sin is greatest, and he replied: “to make a rival for Allah, while He alone created you” — placing this specific sin, shirk, at the very top of the list, worse even than killing one’s own child out of fear of poverty or committing adultery with a neighbour’s wife.

An argument from evidence, addressed to everyone

Ibn Kathir notes that verses 21–22 function as a reasoned argument, not merely a command: contemplating creation — from the ground beneath one’s feet to the rain that produces food — should itself lead a thinking person toward recognising a single, deliberate Creator, making the exclusive worship of Him the only sensible conclusion.

□ Where this comes from

Based on Tafsir Ibn Kathir’s commentary on Surah al-Baqarah 2:21–22, including the hadith he cites (found in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim) where the Prophet (pbuh) names shirk — making a rival for Allah who alone created a person — as the greatest of all sins.

□ Elaborate

This passage builds its case for worshipping Allah entirely from things people experience every day — solid ground, breathable sky, rain, food — rather than from a miracle or a single dramatic event. Why might everyday, repeated evidence be a more powerful (or a weaker) argument than a single spectacular sign? Give your own view.

PAST PAPER · 0493/11 · PAPER 1, Q1 · OCT/NOV 2019, MAY/JUNE 2021, OCT/NOV 2024

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) Briefly describe the main theme(s) in each passage. 4 marks (b) Briefly explain the importance of these themes in a Muslim’s life today. 4 marks

AO1 · model answer points

  1. A direct call to all mankind to worship Allah because He is their Creator.
  2. Allah’s provision for humanity — a stable earth, a protective sky, rain, and food.
  3. A clear warning against shirk — setting up rivals to Allah while knowing the truth.

Part (b) · AO2 — importance today

Encourages daily gratitude (shukr) by linking ordinary things (food, weather, land) directly back to Allah; reinforces that Tawheed is not just a belief but a response to visible evidence, relevant whenever a Muslim reflects on nature; the hadith on shirk being the greatest sin gives this passage real weight when explaining why Muslims take avoiding shirk so seriously.

Mark scheme wording · verbatim (CIE 0493/11 MS, May/June 2021 & Oct/Nov 2019)

(a) “God is the Creator of humankind so they should only worship Him” and “Humankind should not commit the greatest sin of associating partners/rivals with God” — also framed as “God’s relationship with the created world: God as Creator and Sustainer; being grateful to God.” (b) “The natural world is a comfort for humankind, and so they should look after it,” and “God provides the world for humankind so humans should be responsible for looking after their environment.”

Preparation note (study tip, not from an official examiner report): Candidates sometimes describe verse 22 purely as “a nature description” without connecting it back to the command to worship in verse 21 — the mark scheme wants the logical link (creation and provision → therefore worship, and stewardship of the environment) made explicit, not the two verses treated separately.
