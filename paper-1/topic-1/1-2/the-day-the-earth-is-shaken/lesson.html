The Day the Earth is Shaken

1.2 Allah’s Relationship with the Created World · Surah az-Zalzalah 99:1–8

On the Last Day, this surah says, the earth itself will speak — and no deed, however small, will be too insignificant to be seen.

Surah az-Zalzalah (99) is a short, vivid description of the earthquake of the Last Day and its consequence: total, inescapable accountability.

إِذَا زُلْزِلَتِ الْأَرْضُ زِلْزَالَهَا · وَأَخْرَجَتِ الْأَرْضُ أَثْقَالَهَا · وَقَالَ الْإِنسَانُ مَا لَهَا · يَوْمَئِذٍ تُحَدِّثُ أَخْبَارَهَا · بِأَنَّ رَبَّكَ أَوْحَىٰ لَهَا · يَوْمَئِذٍ يَصْدُرُ النَّاسُ أَشْتَاتًا لِّيُرَوْا أَعْمَالَهُمْ · فَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ خَيْرًا يَرَهُ · وَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ شَرًّا يَرَهُ “When the earth is shaken with its [final] earthquake, and the earth throws out its burdens, and man says, ‘What is with it?’ — that Day it will report its news, because your Lord has inspired it. That Day mankind will proceed in scattered groups to be shown their deeds. So whoever does an atom’s weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom’s weight of evil will see it.” Qur’an 99:1–8 — Arabic given for reference only; the exam assesses the English translation, not Arabic recall

The earthquake and the earth’s burdens

Ibn Kathir explains that when the earth is shaken with its “violent shaking,” it will be fundamentally transformed — thrown into convulsions unlike anything experienced before. The earth will then “throw forth that which is in it” — its contents, including the dead of every generation and anything buried within it, like precious metals.

The earth “speaks”

The surah then says the earth will report “her news” (akhbaraha) — commonly understood as the earth testifying about everything that happened on its surface. Ibn Kathir cites a hadith in which the Prophet (pbuh), asked to explain this, said the earth’s “information is that it will testify against every male and female servant, about what they did upon its surface” — recounting, in effect, exactly what each person did and where.

Every atom’s weight, seen

The surah closes with its most famous line: “Whoever does an atom’s weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom’s weight of evil will see it.” Ibn Kathir stresses that no action is insignificant on that Day — not the smallest act of kindness, and not the smallest wrongdoing — and reinforces this with hadith warning against belittling small sins (assuming they don’t matter) or undervaluing small good deeds (assuming they’re not worth doing), since they all accumulate and will be shown.

Unpacking Surah az-Zalzalah for an exam answer

1

The earthquake and the earth throwing out its contents

A description of the physical upheaval of the Last Day.

2

The earth testifying

Every human deed done on its surface is reported, by Allah’s command.

3

The atom’s-weight principle

Total accountability, with no deed — good or bad — too small to count.

□ Where this comes from

Based on Tafsir Ibn Kathir’s commentary on Surah az-Zalzalah, including the hadith he cites in which the Prophet (pbuh) explains that the earth’s “testimony” is a report of every deed done upon it by every person.

□ Elaborate

This surah’s most quoted line is about deeds “the weight of an atom” — the smallest possible measure. Why might the Qur’an choose the smallest possible unit, rather than saying simply “every deed will be seen,” to make its point about accountability? What difference does that specific choice of wording make to how a person might behave in small, everyday moments?

PAST PAPER · 0493/11 · PAPER 1, Q1 · OCT/NOV 2017, OCT/NOV 2019, MAY/JUNE 2022, OCT/NOV 2025

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) Briefly describe the main theme(s) in each passage. 4 marks (b) Briefly explain the importance of these themes in a Muslim’s life today. 4 marks

AO1 · model answer points

  1. The earthquake of the Last Day, and the earth throwing out its contents.
  2. The earth testifying/reporting everything that was done upon it, by Allah’s command.
  3. Total accountability — even an atom’s weight of good or evil will be seen.

Part (b) · AO2 — importance today

Encourages Muslims to take even small acts of kindness seriously (nothing good is wasted) and to avoid dismissing small sins as unimportant; builds constant awareness of accountability (Akhirah) in everyday behaviour, not just major life decisions; often used to encourage regular small charity and good deeds, since none are considered too minor to matter.

Mark scheme wording · verbatim (CIE 0493/11 MS, Oct/Nov 2019)

(a) “The main theme is God’s relationship with the created world: God’s Power; the Last Day; responsibility for actions; justice for all.” (b) “The last Day creates a fear of accountability, reminding Muslims to stay on the straight path,” with benefit found in “encouragement toward good deeds… and finding comfort in divine justice.”

Preparation note (study tip, not from an official examiner report): Candidates sometimes write generally about “the Day of Judgement” without mentioning this surah’s two distinctive details — the earth’s testimony and the atom’s-weight principle — both of which the mark scheme specifically rewards as this passage’s unique content.
