“Read” — The First Revelation

1.2 Allah’s Relationship with the Created World · Paper 1, Topic 1

The very first word Allah ever revealed to humanity’s final Prophet wasn’t a law, a warning, or a story — it was a command to read, addressed to a man who could not read at all.

Surah al-Alaq, verses 1–5, are the first verses ever revealed of the Qur’an, given to the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh) in the Cave of Hira.

Passage in Arabic — for reference only, not required for the exam

اقْرَأْ بِاسْمِ رَبِّكَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ (١) خَلَقَ الْإِنسَانَ مِنْ عَلَقٍ (٢) اقْرَأْ وَرَبُّكَ الْأَكْرَمُ (٣) الَّذِي عَلَّمَ بِالْقَلَمِ (٤) عَلَّمَ الْإِنسَانَ مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ (٥) Qur’an 96:1–5 (Uthmani script). The exam sets this passage in both Arabic and English but assesses your answer in English — you do not need to read, write, or transliterate the Arabic to succeed in this topic. It is included here to match the authentic source text.

Below is the English translation this lesson (and the exam) works from.

The occasion of revelation

Ibn Kathir recounts that while the Prophet was alone in the Cave of Hira, the angel Jibreel (Gabriel) appeared and commanded him, “Iqra” — “Read!” The Prophet, unable to read, replied that he could not. Jibreel embraced him tightly and repeated the command three times before reciting the opening verses of this surah. Overwhelmed, the Prophet returned home trembling to his wife Khadijah, who reassured him and took him to her cousin Waraqah ibn Nawfal — a Christian scholar — who confirmed that the visitor was the same angel (“An-Namus”) sent to Musa (Moses), and that a great mission now lay ahead.

“Read in the name of your Lord who created”

Ibn Kathir emphasises that “Iqra bismi Rabbik” ties the very first act of learning directly to Allah’s authority — knowledge does not begin from human effort alone but is framed, from its first mention in the Qur’an, as something granted by Allah.

Created from a clot

“He created man from a clot (’alaq).” Despite this humble biological beginning, humanity is immediately shown as capable of receiving knowledge directly from Allah — establishing human dignity and potential from the very start of revelation.

Teaching by the pen

“Read! And your Lord is the Most Generous, who taught by the pen.” Ibn Kathir notes this highlights three ways knowledge is transmitted — through the intellect, through speech, and through writing — with writing (the pen) singled out as a distinct divine gift, since it allows knowledge to be recorded and passed on beyond a single generation.

Teaching what was not known

The passage closes: “taught man what he did not know.” Ibn Kathir reads this as summing up the whole theme: all human knowledge, however it is gained, ultimately traces back to Allah’s generosity in teaching what would otherwise remain unknown.

Revision-notes checklist (Main Theme + Importance)

This lesson explicitly covers every point from the standard syllabus revision-notes summary for this passage: verses from Surah al-Alaq; the first revelation marking the start of Islam; how Allah created man and provides for his needs; Allah giving humans the ability to gain knowledge; and people’s dependence on their Creator in every aspect of life. On importance: it makes Muslims grateful, knowing how they were created; it teaches that humans are honoured above other beings and should be thankful for that; and it encourages success through study and the pursuit of knowledge.

Virtues of this passage (fada’il, from authentic hadith)

This passage’s significance is historical rather than a stated “reward for reciting” virtue — as the first words of the Qur’an ever revealed, its status is unmatched among the 15 set passages, but no separate hadith promises a specific reward for reciting verses 1–5 on their own.

□ Common mix-up to avoid

Don’t confuse Surah al-Alaq 96:1–5 (the first revelation itself, tested here as a “Major theme” passage) with the fuller narrative of the Cave of Hira told in the Prophet’s biography (seerah) under Topic 3 of this syllabus. For THIS unit, the exam wants the theme of the passage (knowledge as a gift from Allah) — for Topic 3, it wants the sequence of events. Know both, but don’t answer one question with the other’s content.

□ Elaborate

The very first command in the Qur’an is “Read” — not “believe,” “pray,” or “obey.” What do you think it means that Islam’s foundational text opens by valuing knowledge and learning above almost everything else? How might this shape the importance many Muslim communities place on education?

PAST PAPER · 0493/11 · Paper 1, Q1

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) 4 marks · (b) 4 marks

This passage has been offered as one of the three options in: Oct/Nov 2016, May/June 2017, Oct/Nov 2017, Oct/Nov 2020, May/June 2022, May/June 2024, Oct/Nov 2024.

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) Briefly describe the main theme(s) in each passage. [4] (b) Briefly explain the importance of these themes in a Muslim’s life today. [4]

AO1 · part (a) model answer points

  1. The command “Iqra” (Read), tying knowledge directly to Allah’s authority and generosity.
  2. Human creation from a clot (’alaq) — humble origin, but dignified with the capacity for knowledge.
  3. Allah as the One who teaches by the pen, and teaches what was previously unknown.

AO2 · part (b) — importance today

Importance today — this passage is the foundation for the very high value Muslim culture places on literacy and education; reminds Muslims that learning of any kind ultimately depends on Allah’s generosity, not just personal ability; frequently cited to encourage Muslims to seek knowledge, since it is literally the first message Allah ever revealed.

Official mark scheme wording · verbatim, CIE 0493/11 MS, Oct/Nov 2016

(a) “The main themes are: God as Creator; God as the Most Generous; the first revelation/knowledge” — with development on God’s creative power shown through human creation and emphasis on “seeking knowledge to learn about faith and God.” (b) This sura allows humans to “understand how God created them, and how He bestows knowledge upon them,” and encourages “seeking knowledge... especially religious knowledge so they can get to know their Lord,” and being “grateful to God.”

Preparation note (not from an official examiner report — a study tip based on past-paper frequency): This is the single most frequently tested passage in this whole topic (7 out of 20 sessions). Candidates often retell the Cave of Hira story instead of analysing the verses’ theme (knowledge/learning as divine gift) — the mark scheme rewards the theme, with the story used only briefly as context, not as the main answer.
