“Your Lord Has Not Forsaken You” — Surah ad-Duha

1.3 Allah’s Messengers · Paper 1, Topic 1

When revelation paused and disbelievers mocked the Prophet (pbuh) for being abandoned by his Lord, Allah’s answer came not as a rebuke of the mockers, but as a personal, tender reassurance.

Surah ad-Duha (93) was revealed during a difficult period in the Prophet’s life, and directly addresses his distress with comfort, a promise, and a set of commands rooted in his own life story.

The occasion of revelation

Ibn Kathir explains that revelation paused for a period (a “fatrah”), causing the Prophet real distress. Disbelievers used the pause to taunt him, suggesting that his Lord had abandoned him or come to hate him. This surah was revealed as Allah’s direct response to that taunt.

“Your Lord has neither forsaken you nor hated you”

Allah swears by the forenoon (ad-duha) and the still night to open with direct reassurance: the pause in revelation was never abandonment. “And the Hereafter is better for you than the first [life].” Ibn Kathir reads this as a promise extending beyond the immediate situation — not just that revelation would resume, but that ultimate reward in the Hereafter far outweighs any present difficulty.

“Your Lord will give you, and you will be satisfied”

Allah promises the Prophet future favour so complete that he will be well-pleased — understood by scholars to include both the success of his mission in this life and his unique status of intercession in the Hereafter.

Three favours already given

Ibn Kathir details three specific favours Allah reminds the Prophet of, each paired with a command: 1. Found an orphan, and gave shelter — through his grandfather Abdul-Muttalib and then his uncle Abu Talib. 2. Found unaware/without guidance, and guided him — before revelation, he did not know the detailed message he would bring; Allah guided him to it. 3. Found in need, and made self-sufficient — through the blessings of marriage to Khadijah and the growth of his affairs.

The commands that follow

Because of these three favours, the Prophet — and by extension every Muslim — is commanded: do not oppress the orphan; do not repel the one who asks (for help or knowledge); and proclaim the favour of your Lord.

“By the morning brightness, and [by] the night when it covers with darkness, your Lord has not forsaken you, nor has He hated you. And the Hereafter is better for you than the first [life]. And your Lord is going to give you, and you will be satisfied.” Qur’an 93:1–5

The three favours and their matching commands

1

Found an orphan, sheltered

Command: do not oppress the orphan.

2

Found unaware, guided

Command: do not repel the one who asks (implied: share guidance/knowledge generously, as he was guided).

3

Found in need, made self-sufficient

Command: proclaim the favour of your Lord.

□ Where this comes from

Based on Tafsir Ibn Kathir’s commentary on Surah ad-Duha, including his account of the occasion of revelation (the pause in revelation, and the disbelievers’ taunt) and his reading of the three favours (orphanhood, guidance, provision) as directly generating the surah’s closing commands.

□ Elaborate

Each command at the end of this surah is directly linked to something Allah had already done for the Prophet personally (sheltered when an orphan → don’t oppress orphans; guided when unaware → don’t repel those who ask; enriched when in need → proclaim Allah’s favour). Why might grounding a moral command in someone’s own lived experience be more effective than simply commanding “be kind to orphans” without that connection?

PAST PAPER · 0493/11 · Paper 1, Q1

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) 4 marks · (b) 4 marks

This passage has been offered as one of the three options in: May/June 2020, May/June 2024.

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) Briefly describe the main theme(s) in each passage. [4] (b) Briefly explain the importance of these themes in a Muslim’s life today. [4]

AO1 · part (a) model answer points

  1. Allah’s reassurance that He has not forsaken or hated the Prophet (pbuh), during a pause in revelation.
  2. The promise that the Hereafter is better, and that Allah will give the Prophet so he is satisfied.
  3. The three favours (orphaned and sheltered, unaware and guided, in need and made self-sufficient) and the commands that follow from them.

AO2 · part (b) — importance today

Importance today — a source of comfort for Muslims during their own periods of spiritual difficulty or feeling distant from Allah, since even the Prophet experienced this and was reassured; directly grounds practical commands (kindness to orphans, generosity to those who ask, gratitude) in personal experience, encouraging Muslims to let their own hardships shape their compassion for others; models turning personal blessing into a public proclamation of gratitude rather than private complacency.

Examiner note: This is one of the least-tested passages (only 2 sessions in the last decade), so it is often under-prepared — candidates should not skip it simply because it appears less frequently, since when it is set, weak preparation is common and easy marks are available for well-prepared candidates.
