Isa — A Servant and Messenger

1.3 Allah’s Messengers · Paper 1, Topic 1

Every miracle attributed to Isa (as) in this passage — even raising the dead — is followed immediately by the same three words: “by My permission.”

Surah al-Ma’idah, verse 110, is Allah’s own reminder to Isa (Jesus) of the favours given to him — and, at the same time, one of the Qur’an’s clearest statements that Isa was a servant and messenger of Allah, not divine himself.

Strengthened with the Holy Spirit, and speaking in the cradle

Allah reminds Isa: “I strengthened you with the Holy Spirit (Ruh al-Qudus, understood as the angel Jibreel), so you spoke to the people in the cradle and in maturity.” Ibn Kathir notes that speaking as an infant was a miraculous sign given specifically to defend the honour of Isa’s mother, Maryam, against false accusation.

Taught the Book, wisdom, the Torah, and the Gospel

Allah lists further favours: teaching Isa the Book, the Hikmah (wisdom), the Tawrah (Torah), and the Injil (Gospel) — establishing Isa as a recipient of revealed scripture and prophetic wisdom, in the same tradition as the prophets before him.

Miracles, always “by My permission”

The verse then lists specific miracles: creating the shape of a bird from clay and breathing into it so that it became a bird “by My permission,” healing the blind and the leper “by My permission,” and bringing the dead out of their graves “by My permission.” Ibn Kathir stresses that this repeated phrase is deliberate and central to the verse’s meaning: Isa performed no miracle independently or by his own power — every act depended entirely on Allah’s authorisation.

Protected from the Children of Israel

The verse closes by reminding Isa that Allah restrained the Children of Israel from harming him when he came to them with clear proofs, and some among them accused him of clear sorcery.

The theological point

Ibn Kathir concludes that this structure — every extraordinary ability attributed to Isa immediately qualified with “by My permission” — definitively establishes Isa’s status in the Qur’an as a commissioned messenger and honoured servant of Allah, not an independent divine figure.

“[Remember] when Allah will say, ’O Isa, son of Maryam, remember My favour upon you... when I strengthened you with the Holy Spirit... and when you made from clay a form like that of a bird, by My permission, and you breathed into it, and it became a bird, by My permission. And you healed the blind and the leper by My permission, and you brought forth the dead by My permission.’” Qur’an 5:110

What this passage affirms about Isa (as)

  • A servant and messenger of Allah, honoured and given real miracles
  • Given revelation — the Book, wisdom, Torah, and Gospel
  • Protected by Allah from those who sought to harm him

What this passage rules out

  • That Isa performed any miracle by his own independent power
  • That Isa is divine or shares in Allah’s power (“by My permission” repeated for every miracle)
  • That Isa’s status differs in kind from the other prophets, rather than in the specific miracles given to him

□ Where this comes from

Based on Tafsir Ibn Kathir’s commentary on Surah al-Ma’idah 5:110, which reads the repeated phrase “by My permission” as the verse’s central theological point — every miracle attributed to Isa is explicitly and repeatedly tied back to Allah’s authorisation alone.

□ Elaborate

This verse could have simply listed Isa’s miracles without repeating “by My permission” each time — yet it repeats the phrase for nearly every miracle mentioned. What effect does that repetition have on a reader, and why might repeating it be more effective than stating it only once at the start?

PAST PAPER · 0493/11 · Paper 1, Q1

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) 4 marks · (b) 4 marks

This passage has been offered as one of the three options in: Oct/Nov 2018, Oct/Nov 2019, Oct/Nov 2022, May/June 2024.

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) Briefly describe the main theme(s) in each passage. [4] (b) Briefly explain the importance of these themes in a Muslim’s life today. [4]

AO1 · part (a) model answer points

  1. Isa strengthened with the Holy Spirit and speaking from the cradle, and taught the Book, wisdom, Torah, and Gospel.
  2. Isa’s miracles (the clay bird, healing, raising the dead) — each one explicitly “by My permission.”
  3. Allah’s protection of Isa from the Children of Israel who sought to harm him.

AO2 · part (b) — importance today

Importance today — foundational to the Muslim belief that Isa is a beloved and honoured prophet but not divine, shaping respectful yet clear Muslim positions in interfaith dialogue with Christians; reassures Muslims that even the most extraordinary miracles always depend on Allah’s will, not human or angelic power, reinforcing Tawheed; models honouring a prophet fully while never crossing into worshipping him.

Examiner note: Candidates sometimes describe Isa’s miracles without stating the “by My permission” detail, which is the specific theological point examiners are looking for — simply listing the miracles without this qualifier misses the passage’s central theme.
