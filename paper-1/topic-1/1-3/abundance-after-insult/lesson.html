Abundance After Insult — Surah al-Kawthar

1.3 Allah’s Messengers · Paper 1, Topic 1

When the Prophet’s enemies mocked him for having no surviving sons and predicted he would be forgotten, the Qur’an’s shortest surah answered them with three verses — and turned their insult back on themselves.

Surah al-Kawthar (108), the shortest surah in the Qur’an at just three verses, was revealed to comfort and vindicate the Prophet (pbuh) against a specific, painful taunt.

The occasion of revelation: “abtar”

Ibn Kathir explains that after the Prophet’s sons died in infancy, the Quraysh mocked him, calling him “abtar” — cut off, without a male heir — implying his message and legacy would end with him and be forgotten once he died.

“We have given you al-Kawthar”

Allah’s response opens: “Indeed, We have given you al-Kawthar.” Ibn Kathir explains Kawthar means abundant goodness, and identifies it specifically with a magnificent river in Paradise granted to the Prophet — described elsewhere in hadith as having banks of gold and pearls, with water whiter than milk and sweeter than honey — while also encompassing the wider abundance of blessings, followers, and lasting influence given to him.

The command: pray and sacrifice for your Lord alone

Verse 2 instructs: “So pray to your Lord and sacrifice [to Him alone].” Ibn Kathir reads this as a direct contrast with idolaters’ practice of dedicating prayer and sacrifice to false gods — the Prophet, given such abundance, is commanded to direct his worship exclusively to the One who gave it.

“Indeed, your enemy is the one cut off”

The surah closes by reversing the insult entirely: “Indeed, your enemy — he is the one cut off.” Ibn Kathir explains that it is not the Prophet whose legacy ends, but his enemies’ — while the Prophet’s name, message, and remembrance are preserved by Allah and followed by believers until the Day of Judgement.

□ Where this comes from

Based on Tafsir Ibn Kathir’s commentary on Surah al-Kawthar, including the occasion of revelation (the Quraysh’s “abtar” taunt) and his identification of al-Kawthar with the river in Paradise as well as wider abundant blessing.

□ Elaborate

This surah answers a personal insult (being called “abtar”) not by arguing directly against the claim, but by describing what Allah has given the Prophet and then reversing the insult onto his enemies. Why might responding this way — with abundance and reversal, rather than direct denial — be a more powerful answer to an insult?

Choose any two of the following passages from the Qur’an. (a) Briefly describe the main theme(s) in each passage. [4] (b) Briefly explain the importance of these themes in a Muslim’s life today. [4]

AO1 · part (a) model answer points

  1. The occasion of revelation — the Quraysh’s mockery of the Prophet as “abtar” after his sons died.
  2. Allah’s gift of al-Kawthar — abundant good, including the river in Paradise.
  3. The command to pray and sacrifice to Allah alone, and the reversal of the insult onto the Prophet’s enemies.

AO2 · part (b) — importance today

Importance today — a source of comfort for Muslims facing loss, mockery, or apparent setbacks, showing that Allah’s response to hardship can be abundance rather than mere consolation; reinforces that worship (prayer and sacrifice) belongs to Allah alone, even amid personal grief; the Prophet’s legacy being preserved rather than “cut off” is cited whenever Muslims reflect on the continued global spread of his message, centuries later.

Examiner note: Candidates sometimes miss the occasion of revelation (the “abtar” taunt) entirely, describing only “Allah gave the Prophet a river in Paradise” — examiners reward candidates who explain WHY the surah was revealed, since that context is essential to understanding its full theme.
