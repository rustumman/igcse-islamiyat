## LESSON: Birth and Early Childhood
Lesson slug: birth-and-early-childhood

### Lede
*Three losses before the age of eight — a father he never met, a mother he lost on a desert road, a grandfather who died holding his hand — shaped the boy who would one day be trusted by the whole of Makkah. This is the part of the seerah examiners return to again and again, so get the sequence and the names exact.*

### Content
**Muhammad ibn Abdullah** was born into the **Banu Hashim** clan of the **Quraysh**, the leading tribe of Makkah. His genealogy was recorded with unusual care by later scholars: an unbroken chain of twenty generations back to Adnan, tracing through ancestors such as **Qusayy** (who first established Quraysh political control of Makkah), **Hashim** (who organised the clan's great trading caravans, the "Rihlat al-Shita' wa al-Sayf" — the winter and summer journeys), and **Abdul Muttalib**, the Prophet's own grandfather, who famously rediscovered the buried well of **Zamzam**.

He was born in the same year that **Abraha**, the Christian governor of Yemen, marched on Makkah with an army supported by an elephant, intending to destroy the Kaabah. His forces were destroyed before they could reach the sanctuary — an episode the Qur'an itself references in Surah al-Fil ("The Elephant") — and that year became known across Arabia as the **Year of the Elephant**, traditionally dated to around 570–571 CE. The Prophet's own birth is remembered as having taken place on a Monday, at midday; the precise date within the month of **Rabi al-Awwal** is disputed among early biographers — Ibn Ishaq recorded the **12th**, while other early sources suggest the 2nd, 8th or 10th. Most exam answers can safely state "12th Rabi al-Awwal, 571 CE" as the traditional date, while a stronger answer can note that the exact day is not settled with certainty.

#### An Orphan Before Birth
His father, **Abdullah ibn Abdul Muttalib**, had married **Aminah bint Wahb** — daughter of the chief of the Banu Zuhrah clan — only shortly before setting out with a trading caravan to Syria. On the return journey he fell seriously ill and stayed behind with relatives in **Yathrib** (later Madinah), where he died, still a young man. He never lived to see his son born. Aminah, still a young widow, carried and gave birth to Muhammad alone.

On the seventh day after the birth, his grandfather **Abdul Muttalib** held a naming feast and had the child circumcised, as was customary. He chose the unusual name **Muhammad**, meaning "the praised one," explaining his choice with the words: "I want him to be praised by the people of the earth as I want him to be praised by the people of the heavens."

#### Fostered in the Desert: Halimah al-Sa'diyyah
Following Quraysh custom, noble Makkan families sent their infants out to be nursed by Bedouin women, away from the unhealthy air of the city — this built physical stamina, kept the child from being over-indulged, and, importantly, meant the child grew up speaking the purest classical Arabic, for which the desert tribe of **Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr** was especially renowned. A woman of that tribe, **Halimah al-Sa'diyyah**, took the infant Muhammad into her care. She had at first been reluctant to take an orphan, since orphans could not be expected to bring the same reward from a wealthy father, but her husband urged her: "Allah will bless us through him." He was right — accounts describe an old, dry goat that began producing milk after the child's arrival, and a slow mount that suddenly became the fastest in the caravan. Halimah found the household's fortunes so improved that she kept finding reasons to extend the stay; what was meant to last a short while stretched to around two years.

#### Return, and the Death of Aminah at al-Abwa
Muhammad was returned to his mother Aminah at around the age of six. Soon afterwards, Aminah took him on a journey to Yathrib, accompanied by the family's servant, **Umm Ayman**, to visit relatives of his late father. On the return journey to Makkah, Aminah fell ill and died at a place called **al-Abwa**, between Makkah and Yathrib. Umm Ayman brought the child safely back to Makkah. He was not yet seven years old, and had now lost both parents.

#### Grandfather Abdul Muttalib, then Uncle Abu Talib
Custody passed to his grandfather, **Abdul Muttalib**, by then elderly and blind, but who showed the boy particular affection — he alone among all the grandchildren was permitted to sit on Abdul Muttalib's own reserved seating mat beside the Kaabah, and when the boy once went missing while searching for lost camels, his grandfather was frantic with worry and declared he would never again let him out of his sight. This second guardianship, too, was brief: Abdul Muttalib died when Muhammad was **eight years old**. On his deathbed, he entrusted the boy to his son **Abu Talib**, full brother of the Prophet's late father Abdullah. Abu Talib would go on to raise, protect and support his nephew for decades to come, well into the years of prophethood itself.

By the age of eight, then, Muhammad had lost his father before birth, his mother at around six, and his grandfather at eight — raised in succession by his mother, his foster-mother, his grandfather, and finally his uncle. The Qur'an later addressed this directly.

### Pull Quote
> "Did He not find you an orphan and shelter you? Did He not find you lost and guide you? Did He not find you in need and make you self-sufficient?"
> — Qur'an 93:6–8 (Surah ad-Duha)

### Timeline
- **c. 570–571 CE (Year of the Elephant)** — Birth in Makkah — Born into Banu Hashim, a posthumous son of Abdullah; grandfather Abdul Muttalib names him Muhammad on the seventh day.
- **Infancy – age ~6** — Fostered by Halimah al-Sa'diyyah — Sent to Banu Sa'd in the desert for around two years, then remained in Makkah with his mother until about age six.
- **Age ~6** — Death of Aminah at al-Abwa — Mother dies returning from a visit to relatives in Yathrib; Umm Ayman brings him back to Makkah.
- **Age 6–8** — Raised by Abdul Muttalib — Grandfather cares for him with special affection until his own death.
- **Age 8 onward** — Raised by Abu Talib — Uncle takes over guardianship and support, continuing long into the prophetic mission.

### Source Box
Label: Common mix-up to avoid
Text: Don't confuse the two "guardian" grandparents/uncles. **Abdul Muttalib** (grandfather) cared for him from about age 6 to age 8. **Abu Talib** (uncle, Abdul Muttalib's son and Abdullah's full brother) took over from age 8 onward — it is Abu Talib, not Abdul Muttalib, who was still alive and protecting the Prophet during the early years of revelation in Makkah, which you will meet again in Unit 3.4.

Question: (a) Give an account of the Prophet's life from his birth until he received revelation. [10]
AO1 model answer points:
1. Born in Makkah into Banu Hashim (Quraysh), Year of the Elephant, traditionally 12th Rabi al-Awwal 571 CE; father Abdullah died before his birth, mother Aminah bint Wahb.
2. Named Muhammad by grandfather Abdul Muttalib on the seventh day; fostered by Halimah al-Sa'diyyah of Banu Sa'd in the desert for about two years, as was Quraysh custom.
3. Mother Aminah died at al-Abwa when he was about six, returning from Yathrib; Umm Ayman brought him back to Makkah.
4. Raised by grandfather Abdul Muttalib until his death when Muhammad was eight, then by uncle Abu Talib.
5. Worked as a shepherd in his youth; travelled with trade caravans and, later, undertook a profitable trading journey to Syria for the wealthy widow Khadijah bint Khuwaylid.
6. Witnessed and took a peripheral part in the Harb al-Fijar as a teenager, and was present at the signing of the Hilf al-Fudul pact protecting the wronged.
7. Married Khadijah at age 25 (she was around 40); they had six children together, two sons (who died in infancy) and four daughters.
8. At about age 35 helped rebuild the flood-damaged Kaabah and resolved the Quraysh dispute over placing the Black Stone.
9. Developed a habit of withdrawing to the cave of Hira on Jabal al-Nur to reflect, in the years just before revelation began.
10. Received the first revelation at age 40 through the angel Jibril in the cave of Hira (covered in full in Unit 3.2).

### Exam Lab
Past paper reference: J21 s11 / 0493/11 / Q14(b); J22 s11 / 0493/11 / Q14(b)
Sessions seen: June 2021 (0493/11), June 2022 (0493/11)
Question: (b) The Prophet was an orphan who was raised by many different people. How did this help prepare him for prophethood? [4]
AO1 model answer points:
1. Losing his father, mother and grandfather in quick succession gave him early experience of hardship and loss, building patience and resilience he would need to endure the difficulties of prophethood.
2. Being cared for by different households (mother, foster-mother, grandfather, uncle) meant he grew up close to people of very different circumstances — desert Bedouin and Makkan noble alike — giving him empathy and understanding for people from all walks of life.
3. Having no father to inherit wealth or status from, he learned self-reliance and humility rather than pride, qualities that made his later message of equality and humility credible.
4. His personal experience of being an orphan gave him particular compassion for the vulnerable, orphans and the poor — a concern reflected throughout the Qur'an and his own teachings after prophethood.
AO2 part (b), if any: (this question is itself the AO2 part — answer by linking each stage of his upbringing to a quality it built, rather than simply retelling the biography again).
Examiner note: This is an AO2 (evaluation/application) question, not a retelling of part (a). Do not simply repeat the biography — each point must explicitly explain *how* a hardship *helped*. Strong answers often quote or reference Qur'an 93:6–8, which directly frames his orphanhood as something Allah turned into shelter and guidance.
