## LESSON: Persecution of the Weak and the Prophet
Lesson slug: persecution-of-the-weak-and-the-prophet

### Lede
*When words failed to silence the new message, Quraysh turned to torture — targeting first the poorest and most defenceless believers, then the Prophet himself, in a campaign of cruelty that early Muslims would remember, and be tested by, for the rest of their lives.*

### Predict Box

### Content
Once Islam was preached openly, Quraysh opposition escalated from mockery into physical violence — and the earliest victims were those with the least protection. In Makkah's clan-based society, a person's safety depended heavily on their tribe's willingness to defend them. Enslaved people and the very poor had no such protection, which made them the first targets of organised persecution.

#### Bilal ibn Rabah

**Bilal ibn Rabah al-Habashi**, an enslaved convert, belonged to **Umayyah ibn Khalaf**, one of the most bitter enemies of the new faith. Umayyah dragged Bilal through the streets of Makkah with a rope around his neck and, in the full heat of the desert sun, pinned him beneath massive heated rocks — stones so hot, it was said, that meat could be cooked on them. Despite this torment, Bilal refused to renounce his faith, repeating only one phrase over and over: **"Ahad, Ahad" — "One, One,"** affirming the oneness of Allah even as he was tortured for it. Bilal survived, was eventually freed (through Abu Bakr, as covered in earlier units), and went on to become the Prophet's official **muezzin**, the one chosen to call the adhan — his voice transformed from a cry of suffering into the call to prayer heard by the whole Muslim community.

#### Sumayyah bint Khayyat and Yasir — the First Martyrs

**Yasir** and his wife **Sumayyah bint Khayyat**, along with their son **Ammar**, were enslaved converts belonging to a hostile Makkan master. Quraysh subjected the whole family to brutal torture in an attempt to force them to recant. Sumayyah was killed by **Abu Jahl** himself, making her the **first martyr (shahida) of Islam**. Yasir also died from his torture shortly after. Their deaths were witnessed by their teenage son Ammar, who was forced to watch his parents killed in front of him for refusing to abandon their faith — a detail that makes the family's story one of the most solemn in the whole unit, and one commonly tested in exam questions on persecution.

#### Ammar ibn Yasir

**Ammar ibn Yasir**, only around **fifteen years old** at the time, was tortured alongside his parents. Overwhelmed by the brutality he had witnessed and endured, he eventually spoke words of disbelief under duress simply to make the torture stop. Distressed, he came to the Prophet fearing he had committed a grave sin. The Prophet reassured him, and a Qur'anic verse (16:106) was later understood to address situations exactly like his — permitting an outward statement of disbelief under compulsion, so long as the heart remains firm in faith. Ammar went on to become one of the most respected and long-serving Companions, eventually dying in battle decades later (37 AH, at the Battle of Siffin) as an old man still serving the Muslim community.

#### Khabbab ibn al-Aratt

**Khabbab ibn al-Aratt** belonged to a female slave-owner and was subjected to severe beatings. His owner had heated iron — the kind used for forging swords — pressed directly onto his back, leaving permanent scarring. Decades later, during the caliphate of Umar, Khabbab showed his scarred back as proof of what he had endured for his faith, and was honoured with a position and salary from the state treasury, much of which he shared generously with the needy. Even on his deathbed, tradition holds that Khabbab wept — not from fear of death or from pain, but from concern that the comfort and prosperity he had lived to see might reduce his reward in comparison to companions who had died earlier, before Islam became secure, without ever tasting such ease.

#### Direct Persecution of the Prophet Himself

The Prophet's own status as a member of Banu Hashim gave him some protection — Quraysh could not kill him outright without risking a blood feud with his clan, protected as he was for a time by his uncle Abu Talib — but this did not stop repeated attempts at humiliation, mockery, and physical assault.

**Mockery** was constant: he was branded majnun (madman), sahir (sorcerer), and sha'ir (poet) — labels designed to dismiss his message without engaging with it. Beyond words, the Quraysh subjected him to real physical danger and emotional cruelty.

##### The Strangling at the Ka'bah

On one occasion, while the Prophet was praying at the Ka'bah, **Uqbah ibn Abi Mu'ayt** seized the Prophet's own garment and wound it around his neck, choking him. **Abu Bakr** rushed to intervene, pulling the attacker away and confronting him directly:

> "Are you going to kill a person just because he says, 'My Lord is Allah'?!"
> — Abu Bakr, defending the Prophet at the Ka'bah

##### Entrails Thrown During Prostration

In another notorious incident, while the Prophet was prostrating in prayer near the Ka'bah, Uqbah ibn Abi Mu'ayt again targeted him — this time dumping the entrails of a slaughtered camel onto his back while he remained in prostration, so that the weight prevented him from rising. His young daughter **Fatimah** rushed out to remove the filth from him. After completing his prayer, the Prophet invoked a specific supplication against seven named individuals responsible for the abuse — a supplication tradition holds was answered years later, when all seven men were killed at the **Battle of Badr**.

##### Abu Jahl's Failed Attempt

On a separate occasion, **Abu Jahl** attempted to step on the Prophet's neck while he prayed, but was reported to have suddenly retreated in fear, later explaining that he had seen something terrifying — a pit of fire, with wings hovering above it — a moment tradition presents as divine protection extended to the Prophet at a moment of direct physical danger.

##### His Reply to Abu Talib

As pressure from Quraysh mounted on his uncle **Abu Talib** to hand the Prophet over or force him to stop preaching, Abu Talib appealed to his nephew to ease the situation. The Prophet's reply is one of the most quoted lines in the whole seerah, expressing his total resolve to continue his mission regardless of personal cost:

> "...even if they placed the sun in my right hand and the moon in my left, [asking me] to abandon this course, until Allah has made it victorious, or I perish therein, I would not abandon it."
> — The Prophet's reply to Abu Talib

This single statement is frequently used in exam answers to demonstrate the Prophet's unwavering commitment to his message under the most severe personal pressure, and it marks the point at which further compromise with Quraysh became impossible.
