## LESSON: Farewell Hajj and Death
Lesson slug: farewell-hajj-and-death

### Lede
*A hundred thousand pilgrims gather to watch the Prophet (pbuh) perform the one Hajj of his life as a free Muslim leader — and to hear, on the plain of Arafah, a sermon that would outlive every empire of its age. Two months later, he is gone.*

### Content
#### Setting the stage
After the Conquest of Makkah, the Prophet did not immediately perform Hajj — security concerns around the still-hostile **Thaqif** delayed it. In **9 AH**, after Tabuk and Thaqif's eventual conversion, the Prophet sent **Abu Bakr** to lead roughly 300 Muslims on Hajj, but did not go himself, because the pre-Islamic practice of pagan pilgrims performing **naked tawaf** around the Ka'bah was still ongoing that year, and he chose not to attend a Hajj mixed with such practice. During this Hajj, **Ali ibn Abi Talib** was sent to recite the opening of **Surah at-Tawbah**, announcing that a four-month grace period would apply to existing treaties, that naked tawaf was now prohibited, and — decisively — that **polytheists would be permanently barred from performing Hajj from the following year onward**.

#### The Farewell Hajj, 10 AH
With Arabia now overwhelmingly Muslim, the Prophet departed Madinah on the **25th of Dhu al-Qa'dah, 10 AH**, arriving in Makkah on the **4th of Dhu al-Hijjah**. This was the **only Hajj he ever performed as a Muslim leader**, and estimates suggest over **100,000 pilgrims** accompanied him — the Companion **Jabir ibn Abdillah** described looking in every direction and seeing nothing but riders and pedestrians as far as the eye could see. The Prophet's simple instruction — "**take your rites of Hajj from me**" — established, through his own personal example alone, virtually the entire structure of Islamic pilgrimage practice still followed today.

#### The Farewell Sermon (Khutbat al-Wada')
On the **Day of Arafah**, the Prophet delivered what is remembered as the **Farewell Sermon**, addressing the enormous assembled crowd with a set of principles that reshaped Arabian society at its foundations:

- **Sanctity of life and property.** "Your blood and your money are sacred to you, just as this day, this month, and this land are sacred."
- **Abolition of pre-Islamic usury (riba) and blood feuds.** He announced that all interest-based debts and inherited blood feuds from the Age of Ignorance (Jahiliyyah) were cancelled — deliberately starting with his own family, abolishing the riba owed to his uncle al-Abbas and the blood-claims of his own clan first, to demonstrate the rule applied to everyone equally.
- **Kind treatment of women.** "Fear Allah with regard to women, for you have taken them as a trust under Allah's protection." He reminded the men that women had rights over them just as the men had rights over their wives.
- **Racial equality.** In one of the sermon's most striking lines: "There is no superiority of an Arab over a non-Arab, nor of a non-Arab over an Arab, nor of a white person over a black person, nor a black person over a white person, except by taqwa [God-consciousness]."
- **Muslim brotherhood.** He warned strongly against Muslims returning to violence against one another after his death, insisting on unity above tribal division.
- **Sources of guidance.** He told the assembled Muslims he was leaving them something that, if held firmly, would keep them from ever going astray — the **Book of Allah** — instructing them also to be mindful in how they treated his own family and household.
- **Completion of the message.** When the crowd testified that he had indeed delivered his message faithfully, he raised his hands and called out three times, "Allahumma fashhad" ("O Allah, bear witness") — formally invoking Allah as witness that his mission had been completed.

The completion of the message was matched by a direct Qur'anic revelation on this same occasion:

> "This day I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favour upon you and have approved for you Islam as religion."
> — Qur'an 5:3

#### Return to Madinah and final illness
The Prophet returned to Madinah after completing the Hajj rites. Roughly **two months later**, he began to suffer a severe fever — Aisha later described it as equivalent to the combined fever of ten ordinary people — beginning around the house of his wife Maymuna. As his strength failed, he requested his other wives' permission to remain in **Aisha's house** for the remainder of his illness, and this was granted.

As the illness worsened, the Prophet gave a clear and deliberate instruction: "**Tell Abu Bakr to lead the people in prayer**." This marked the first public sign of who would guide the community once he was gone, and Abu Bakr duly led the congregational prayers during the Prophet's final days. On what would be his final public appearance, at Fajr prayer, the Prophet lifted the curtain of his room to look out at the assembled worshippers one last time; the Companion Anas ibn Malik recalled the moment vividly as one of quiet joy, not yet knowing it would be their final sight of him.

#### Death
The Prophet (pbuh) died on **Monday, 12th Rabi al-Awwal, 11 AH (632 CE)**, in the room of his wife **Aisha**, his head resting in her lap. Among his final acts, he asked repeatedly that a small remaining sum of money — a handful of silver dirhams — be given away to the poor before he died, unwilling to leave this world with unspent wealth that could have helped someone in need.

#### Abu Bakr's response
News of the Prophet's death caused profound shock; **Umar ibn al-Khattab**, in his grief, initially refused to believe it and threatened anyone who claimed it. **Abu Bakr**, arriving and understanding the danger this confusion posed to the young community, addressed the gathered Muslims with words that steadied the entire ummah at its most fragile moment:

> "Whoever used to worship Muhammad, let him know that Muhammad has died. But whoever worships Allah, let him know that Allah is alive and does not die."

He then recited the same verse revealed years earlier after the Prophet's injuries at Uhud, when a similar false rumour of his death had spread:

> "Muhammad is not but a messenger. [Other] messengers have passed on before him. So if he was to die or be killed, would you turn back on your heels [to unbelief]?"
> — Qur'an 3:144

Hearing this recitation, the community is said to have realised, as if for the first time, that the verse had truly been revealed — bringing the shock of grief into acceptance.

#### Burial
The Prophet's body was washed and shrouded, and — following his own earlier teaching that prophets are buried where they die — he was buried beneath the very room in Aisha's house where he had passed away. Muslims came in successive groups over the following two days to pray individually over him, as no single leader conducted one collective funeral prayer, before he was finally laid to rest.

### Timeline
- **9 AH** — Abu Bakr leads Hajj without the Prophet; Ali announces the barring of polytheists from future Hajj.
- **25th Dhu al-Qa'dah, 10 AH** — The Prophet departs Madinah for his one and only Hajj as a Muslim leader.
- **Day of Arafah, 10 AH** — The Farewell Sermon is delivered to over 100,000 pilgrims; Qur'an 5:3 is revealed.
- **Return to Madinah** — The Prophet resumes normal life for roughly two months.
- **Final illness begins** — A severe fever develops; he moves to Aisha's house for his final days.
- **Abu Bakr appointed to lead prayer** — The first clear public sign of coming succession.
- **12th Rabi al-Awwal, 11 AH (632 CE)** — The Prophet dies in Aisha's lap.
- **Abu Bakr's address** — He calms the shocked community, quoting Qur'an 3:144.
- **Burial** — The Prophet is buried beneath the room in which he died, following his own teaching on prophetic burial.

### Source Box
Label: Common mix-up to avoid
Text: Do not confuse the Farewell Hajj (10 AH, the Prophet's only Hajj, featuring the Farewell Sermon) with the Hajj of Abu Bakr the year before (9 AH, which the Prophet did not personally attend). Also be precise that Qur'an 3:144, quoted by Abu Bakr at the Prophet's death, was originally revealed years earlier during the Battle of Uhud — Abu Bakr recited an existing verse to address a strikingly similar situation, he did not receive new revelation at that moment.

AO1 model answer points:
1. Context: departing Madinah on 25th Dhu al-Qa'dah, 10 AH, with over 100,000 pilgrims, the Prophet's only Hajj as Muslim leader, establishing rites "take your rites of Hajj from me."
2. The Farewell Sermon at Arafah: sanctity of life and property; abolition of riba and blood feuds, starting with his own family; kind treatment of women; racial equality regardless of Arab/non-Arab or skin colour; Muslim brotherhood and unity; the Qur'an left as guidance.

4. Qur'an 5:3 revealed on this occasion, declaring the religion perfected.
AO2 part (b), if any: Most important teaching, with reasons. [4] — model approach: select one teaching (e.g. racial equality, or women's rights, or the sanctity of life) and justify why it is most significant, ideally linking it to both its immediate 7th-century context (overturning tribal/racial hierarchies) and its continuing relevance today.
Examiner note: Candidates often list all the sermon's teachings for (a) but fail to select and justify just one clearly for (b) — a strong (b) answer commits to one teaching and explains, with reasoning, why it outweighs the others, rather than repeating the (a) list.
