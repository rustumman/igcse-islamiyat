## LESSON: Rebuilding the Kaabah
Lesson slug: rebuilding-the-kaaba

### Lede
*Five days of stalled construction, four furious clans, and one dispute so dangerous that men had pledged in blood to fight over it — and it was Muhammad, still five years from receiving revelation, who ended it with nothing more than a length of cloth.*

### Predict Box
Imagine four powerful clans, each insisting that theirs alone should have the honour of placing the most sacred object in Makkah back into position. Before reading on: what kind of solution could possibly satisfy all four sides equally?

### Content
#### Fire, Flood, and a Shipwreck
At around **age thirty-five** — roughly **605 CE**, a decade before he would receive the first revelation — Muhammad took part in one of the most consequential events of his pre-prophetic life: the **rebuilding of the Kaabah**. The structure had been damaged first by a fire, thought to have started from nearby cooking embers, and then further weakened by a serious flood that swept through Makkah. The Quraysh agreed the building needed to be properly rebuilt rather than merely patched.

As it happened, the materials for a proper rebuilding arrived almost providentially: a Roman ship, carrying expensive marble and wood intended for a church in Yemen, had wrecked off the coast near **Jeddah**. The Quraysh acquired this cargo, along with the skilled craftsmen who had been aboard, giving them, for the first time, the materials to rebuild the Kaabah to a genuinely elevated standard.

#### The Dispute Over the Black Stone
To manage the work fairly, the Quraysh divided responsibility for rebuilding the different sides of the Kaabah among **four sub-tribes**. This worked smoothly until the project reached its final and most sacred task: setting the **Black Stone (al-Hajar al-Aswad)** back into its corner of the wall. Two of the sub-tribes, **Banu Abd Manaf** and **Banu Makhzum**, each insisted that the honour of placing the stone belonged to them alone. The dispute escalated so seriously that **construction stopped for five days** while the clans argued, and the Banu Makhzum are recorded as having gone so far as to **dip their hands in a bowl of camel blood**, pledging themselves to fight over the matter if necessary. Makkah stood on the edge of serious tribal bloodshed over a single stone.

#### The Solution: A Cloth for Every Clan
It was an elder named **Abu Umayya** who proposed a way out of the deadlock: let the matter be settled by whoever happened to be the very next person to enter the sanctuary through a particular gate, and all sides would accept that person's decision. As fortune had it, the next person through the gate was **Muhammad** — already, by this point in his life, known and trusted across Makkah for his fairness. Each of the rival clans, seeing him approach, felt reassured that he would favour their side.

Muhammad's solution was elegant in its simplicity. He called for a **cloth** to be brought, and instructed **representatives of each of the rival clans to take hold of a corner of it together**. The Black Stone was placed on the cloth, and all the clan leaders lifted it in unison, carrying it to its place at the same height, side by side — so that every clan could rightly say it had taken part in restoring the stone to its position. Muhammad then stepped forward and, with his own hands, lifted the stone from the cloth and set it into the wall of the Kaabah himself.

#### Why This Episode Matters
No single clan lost face; no blood was shed; and the sacred stone was restored to its place with the full and equal participation of everyone who had claimed a stake in the honour. For an exam answer, this episode is one of the clearest pieces of evidence available for Muhammad's character **before** he ever received revelation — it shows creative, practical wisdom, complete impartiality between rival factions, and a natural authority that the whole of Makkah, still years away from any of them becoming Muslim, was willing to accept without argument.

### Timeline
- **Some time before ~605 CE** — Fire and flood damage the Kaabah — The existing structure is weakened and needs full reconstruction.
- **~605 CE (Muhammad, age ~35)** — Roman shipwreck near Jeddah — Marble, wood and craftsmen intended for a Yemenite church become available to the Quraysh.
- **During reconstruction** — Four sub-tribes divide the work — Rebuilding proceeds smoothly until the Black Stone's placement is reached.
- **Five days of deadlock** — Dispute between Banu Abd Manaf and Banu Makhzum — Tension escalates to the point of a blood pledge to fight.
- **Resolution** — Muhammad is the first to enter — Abu Umayya's proposal makes him the arbiter by chance.
- **The cloth method** — All clans lift the stone together — Muhammad places it in the wall himself, satisfying every clan equally.
